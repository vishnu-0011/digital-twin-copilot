1 · Why

The problem we chose

/why.html argues that the gap isn't prediction; it's getting the right person to act in time, with enough trust to stop a running machine.

What the page covers

  • The warning window (P-F interval): a fault becomes detectable at P and causes a breakdown at F. The chart compares acting at F, acting on a calendar, and acting inside the window.
  • Three failure modes: breakdowns found too late, calendars that ignore the machine's condition, and readings that never become decisions (alarm floods get muted).
  • Why an aerospace shop: hard titanium and nickel alloys, long and valuable cuts, bottleneck machines without spares, and written procedures with sign-off.
  • Honest limits: the plant is simulated, and the industry savings figures (US DOE FEMP, 2010) are best cases, not this project's results.
Likely question

"Why not just alarm when vibration crosses a threshold?"

Answer: a fixed limit fires late and says nothing about how long is left. On the test plants the ISO 10816-3 zone C limit catches – of worn readings with a median warning of – cycles; the learned detector warns – cycles ahead. The remaining-life model then turns that into a deadline.

2 · How it works

Architecture, models and decisions

/method.html walks the loop from a vibration reading to a signed-off repair, with the twin's equations and every design choice we rejected.

Five layers, one loop

  • Plant: a SimPy twin of 8 machines; hidden wear drives vibration and temperature with noise. Models never see the wear.
  • Models: an Isolation Forest fitted on the healthy envelope, and a dilated causal TCN (dilations 1-2-4-8, a Chomp1d trim so no future readings leak) predicting cycles to failure.
  • Agents: a LangGraph graph of rules (Monitor → Diagnosis → Scheduler → Report). Diagnosis cites the machine type's procedure; the scheduler drafts a work order when 0.85 × RUL < 30 cycles.
  • People: a supervisor approves; the twin services the machine and its readings return to normal.
Likely question

"Why a TCN rather than an LSTM?"

Answer: convolutions train in parallel across time, residual blocks keep gradients stable, and four dilated layers give a 61-reading receptive field. Gradient boosting is kept as a baseline: the TCN's error is – cycles against –.

3 · Results

What was measured

/results.html is generated by python -m evaluation.run_all, scored on plants the models never saw.

Headline numbers

  • Remaining life: mean error – cycles (gradient boosting –, no-sensor baseline –).
  • Early warning: median – cycles before failure, at the cost of – false alarms on healthy readings.
  • Downtime per shift: – h running to failure, – h with the copilot and no breakdowns.
  • Fixed schedule: tuned on the test plants it reaches – h, slightly better, but at 100% of average life it collapses to – breakdowns.
  • NASA C-MAPSS: built in and run separately (--cmapss-only, about 40 minutes).
Likely question

"How do you know the policy comparison is fair?"

Answer: every policy runs the same – plants with the same random wear, and the copilot uses the real trained model, not the true wear. The fixed schedule is swept and its best setting reported, which flatters it. Repair time is varied from 1.5× to 5× to show the conclusion holds.
